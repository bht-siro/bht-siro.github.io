Greenhouse

We investigate compliant manipulation for harvesting and plant maintenance, where handling requires sufficient grip force while avoiding damage to fruit and stems.

Manipulation in tomato cultivation

Fruit, leaves and stems vary in shape, stiffness and sensitivity to contact. We use these conditions to investigate how mechanical compliance can support grasping and manipulation, and where additional grip force or more precise motion is required.

The greenhouse is one research scenario of our DFG tele.interaction project. The target setup is a TIAGo Pro carrying SIRo Soft Hands, operated through the Visuo-Haptic Input Station. Experiments so far used student-built hands: picking individual fruits succeeded, while de-suckering and defoliation damaged the plants. In two challenge rounds, the tested hands could not generate enough force to close harvesting scissors. Experiments with TIAGo Pro in the greenhouse remain planned.

DFG · Großgeräteinitiative HAW · 533327659 · 07/2024–06/2028 Partner: BHT greenhouse laboratory, Gartenbauliche Phytotechnologie, Department V Target setup: TIAGo Pro + SIRo Soft Hands

The tasks

The target tasks come from commercial tomato cultivation and also form the basis of the student Catch-Up Challenge.

Ausgeizen

De-suckering

Removing lateral shoots from tomato plants to direct growth into the fruit.

Entblatten

Defoliation

Strategic leaf removal to improve light exposure and airflow around the fruit.

Einzelfruchternte

Single-fruit harvest

Picking individual ripe tomatoes without bruising them or the plant.

Rispenernte mit Schere

Cluster harvest

Harvesting whole fruit clusters by cutting the stem with scissors.

The research question

How can a compliant hand adapt to fruit and stems while providing the force required for plant maintenance and tool use? We investigate the contribution of hand mechanics to successful handling and aim to study how visual and haptic feedback support a remote operator working among the plants.
