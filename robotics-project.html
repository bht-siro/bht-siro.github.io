Robotics Project: Soft Robotic Hands

A hands-on project course where bachelor students design, build, and test soft robotic hands, then prove them in real-world challenges against concrete manipulation tasks.

From Lecture Hall to Lab

Within BHT’s bachelor programme in Humanoid Robotics (Humanoide Robotik, HROB), the Robotics Project module offers students the opportunity to apply theoretical knowledge in mechanical design, actuation, and control to a tangible engineering challenge: building a functional soft robotic hand from scratch.

Working in small teams over the course of a semester, students go through the full design cycle: conceptual sketching, CAD modelling, 3D printing and assembly, tendon routing and actuation integration, and finally testing under realistic task conditions. The project is directly embedded in the SIRo Lab’s research on variable-stiffness hands, giving students access to current research hardware, methods and supervision.

The course covers mechanical design and robot construction, robotics fundamentals, and human-robot interaction, all framed around the practical challenge of making a hand that works.

The Challenges

Each year, the project culminates in a competitive challenge where student teams put their soft robotic hands to the test. The challenges are designed to evaluate grasping, manipulation, and task execution under real-world constraints.

2023
ARM Challenge

For the first challenge the tasks were taken directly from the Cybathlon Arm Prosthesis Race and run with student teams. Where Cybathlon pilots compete with powered prostheses, our students competed with hands they had designed and built themselves, on the same everyday manipulation tasks.

  • 1 Grasping and lifting objects of various sizes and weights
  • 2 Manipulating compliant objects (clothing, soft containers)
  • 3 Bimanual coordination tasks (e.g., zipping, hanging)
  • 4 Timed completion: task success as primary metric, speed as secondary

Where the tasks come from

The Cybathlon, organised by ETH Zurich, is a global competition for assistive technology, including powered arm prostheses. Using its task catalogue unchanged gave the student teams an external, established benchmark instead of one we had written ourselves.

2024
Catch-Up Challenge

The second challenge moved into the BHT greenhouse and added the constraint that turned it into a teleoperation problem in miniature. Each team had to build two things: a soft hand, and a controller small enough to be held in one hand. The hand was then operated single-handedly through that controller, on living tomato plants.

That is the same question the lab works on at full scale, where a TIAGo Pro with SIRo Soft Hands is commanded through the Visuo-Haptic Input Station. The students met it with a device they could build in a semester.

  • 1 Ausgeizen (de-suckering): removing lateral shoots from tomato plants to direct growth
  • 2 Entblatten (defoliation): strategic leaf removal to improve light and airflow
  • 3 Einzelfruchternte (single fruit harvest): picking individual ripe tomatoes without damage
  • 4 Rispenernte mit Schere (cluster harvest): harvesting entire fruit clusters by cutting the stem with scissors

These tasks are representative of real challenges in horticultural automation, where soft compliance is essential to avoid crop damage. The name is a play on ketchup, given what the tomatoes usually end up as, and on catching up with what a human hand does among the plants.

What came out of it

  • ✓ Picking single fruits worked. The compliant hands managed it without damaging the tomatoes.
  • ! De-suckering and defoliating did not go well. The plants were damaged in the process.
  • × Cutting clusters failed. The hands could not close the scissors: not enough grip force.
  • × A second attempt in a later semester failed the same way, with hands designed specifically for the scissors. Grip force, not compliance, is where the limit sits.

That last line is the reason the challenges are worth running. A negative result on real plants tells the lab something a simulation would not have.

Learning Outcomes

Through the Robotics Project, students develop competencies across the full engineering spectrum:

  • Mechanical design: CAD, 3D printing, material selection, tendon routing, compliant mechanism design
  • Actuation & control: servo integration, variable-stiffness mechanisms, sensor feedback
  • Experimental methods: task design, performance measurement, iterative improvement
  • Teamwork & communication: collaborative engineering, presentation of results, competitive deadline pressure
  • Research connection: direct exposure to active research problems in soft robotics and manipulation

Context

The Robotics Project is taught as part of the B.Sc. Humanoide Robotik at Berliner Hochschule für Technik. The programme, one of the few bachelor’s degrees in Germany dedicated to humanoid robotics, combines mechanical engineering, electronics, computer science, and human-robot interaction in a practice-oriented curriculum.

Prof. Dr.-Ing. Hannes Höppner leads the SIRo Lab and teaches courses in mechanical design, robot construction, and human-robot interaction within the programme. The lab is located at BHT’s Haus der Robotik.
