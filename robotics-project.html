Robotics Project: Soft Robotic Hands

Student teams design, build and evaluate soft robotic hands in a semester project, testing their designs on everyday manipulation and horticultural tasks.

From concept to experiment

Design, build and evaluate

The Robotics Project is part of BHT’s bachelor programme in Humanoid Robotics (Humanoide Robotik, HROB). Student teams apply mechanical design, actuation and control to the development of a soft robotic hand.

They work through concept development, CAD modelling, 3D printing, assembly, tendon routing and actuation integration. Defined manipulation tasks then help them evaluate their design decisions.

The project connects to SIRo Lab’s research on variable-stiffness hands through access to research hardware, methods and supervision.

Challenge · 2023

ARM Challenge

The first challenge used the task catalogue of the Cybathlon Arm Prosthesis Race. Student teams tackled its everyday manipulation tasks with their own robotic hands.

The tasks

  • Grasping and lifting objects of different sizes and weights
  • Manipulating compliant objects, including clothing and soft containers
  • Bimanual coordination, such as zipping and hanging
  • Timed completion, with task success as the primary metric and speed as secondary

An established benchmark

The Cybathlon, organised by ETH Zurich, is a competition for assistive technology. We adopted its arm-prosthesis task catalogue unchanged.

Challenge · 2024

Catch-Up Challenge

In the BHT greenhouse, teams developed soft robotic hands and controllers that could be operated with one hand. Four tasks on tomato plants combined delicate handling with the force requirements of tool use.

  • De-suckering (Ausgeizen): removing lateral shoots
  • Defoliation (Entblatten): removing leaves to improve light and airflow
  • Single-fruit harvest (Einzelfruchternte): picking ripe tomatoes without damage
  • Cluster harvest (Rispenernte): cutting fruit-cluster stems with scissors

The name “Catch-Up” refers to ketchup and to the challenge of matching human hand capabilities.

What the experiments showed

Fruit harvesting

The tested hands picked individual tomatoes without damaging the fruit.

Plant handling

De-suckering and defoliation caused plant damage, showing the need to refine the handling strategy.

Force for tool use

The hands could not close the scissors. A later challenge round showed the same limitation, including for designs made specifically for this task.

These observations apply to the student-built hands and task configurations tested.

The challenge connects to our research on teleoperated manipulation. Functional integration of TIAGo Pro, initially with one SIRo Soft Hand, and the Visuo-Haptic Input Station is in development. The student devices explore hand design and operator control within the scope of a semester project.

Integration and testing

Putting the designs to work

Testing on a robot connects the hand mechanism to the demands of positioning, actuation and object contact.

Learning outcomes

The project develops practical skills in five areas:

  • Mechanical design: CAD, 3D printing, material selection, tendon routing, compliant mechanism design
  • Actuation & control: servo integration, variable-stiffness mechanisms, sensor feedback
  • Experimental methods: task design, performance measurement, iterative improvement
  • Teamwork & communication: collaborative engineering, project planning, presentation of results
  • Research connection: direct exposure to active research problems in soft robotics and manipulation

Context

The B.Eng. Humanoide Robotik at Berliner Hochschule für Technik combines mechanical engineering, electronics, computer science and human-robot interaction.

Prof. Dr.-Ing. Hannes Höppner leads the SIRo Lab and teaches courses in mechanical design, robot construction, and human-robot interaction within the programme. The lab is located at BHT’s Haus der Robotik.
