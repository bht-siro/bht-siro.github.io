Movement Primitives

How does a robot learn movements from a person, and build tasks from them?

Learning motions for dynamic manipulation

We investigate how robots can learn dynamic skills from human demonstration and adapt them to different tasks. Movement primitives provide compact representations of demonstrated motions.

Throwing is one application context for this research. It connects motion learning with a physical task in which the timing and execution of movement affect the outcome.

Teaching by demonstration

We use human demonstrations to study how a robot can acquire a movement. The video shows an operator guiding TIAGo’s arm through a throwing motion.

An operator demonstrates a throwing motion by guiding TIAGo’s arm.

Current work ICRA 2027 · under review

Our work on Velocity-Aware Via-Point Movement Primitives (V2MP) has been submitted to IEEE ICRA 2027 and is currently under review.

TIAGo performs throwing motions as part of our research on dynamic manipulation.

Research directions

Task instructions

We have begun investigating how language-based instructions could support the use of learned robot skills. This work is at an early stage.

Shared autonomy

We investigate how learned motions could support an operator during teleoperation, combining robot skills with human task guidance.

Beyond throwing

Longer-term research considers other dynamic manipulation tasks and their interaction with variable-stiffness arms and compliant hands.

Platform

We use TIAGo Pro (PAL Robotics) as our physical platform for dynamic manipulation. The throwing demonstrations use the robot’s standard gripper.

Funding

This research uses the TIAGo Pro platform funded through the DFG project tele.interaction (533327659).
