PhD Researcher

Steffen Puhlmann

How much of the control can the hardware itself take over?

Soft robotic hands whose mechanics carry part of the manipulation problem.

Steffen.Puhlmann@bht-berlin.de · ORCID 0000-0002-3821-2127

Research focus

Steffen designed and built the SIRo Soft Hand and is responsible for its further development. It is the oldest of the lab’s platforms and the one every question eventually touches. His guiding question is how much of a manipulation problem the hardware can absorb before a controller has to intervene: which behaviours emerge from the mechanics themselves, and which have to be computed.

Two of his mechanisms are central to the hand. The first is variable stiffness based on Bowden cables, which modulates finger stiffness with inexpensive off-the-shelf components. The second is programmable passive fingertip deformation: the fingertip is designed so that its deformation under load produces the desired grasping behaviour, which makes the material itself part of the computation.

The hand is developed within APPL-FM and is used across the whole lab. It is the manipulation front end in tele.interaction, it handles medication tasks in Robo4Care, and it is the platform being validated for instrument reprocessing in SIRo-AEMP.

Before joining the SIRo Lab, he worked at the Robotics and Biology Laboratory at TU Berlin. There he built the RBO Hand 3, an anthropomorphic soft hand with sixteen independent degrees of actuation, studied why in-hand manipulation with compliant hands is so robust to variation in object shape, weight and speed, and worked on large-scale analyses of human grasping behaviour.
