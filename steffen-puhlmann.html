PhD Researcher

Steffen Puhlmann

How much of the control can the hardware itself take over?

Soft robotic hands with variable stiffness and mechanically programmed fingertip deformation.

Steffen.Puhlmann@bht-berlin.de · ORCID 0000-0002-3821-2127

Research focus

Steffen designed and built the SIRo Soft Hand and leads its further development. He investigates how mechanical design can support grasping and manipulation, and how compliant mechanisms interact with the robot’s controller.

His work includes two mechanisms: variable stiffness based on Bowden cables, using inexpensive standard components, and programmable passive fingertip deformation. By shaping how a fingertip deforms under load, its mechanical structure can contribute to the desired grasping behaviour.

The hand is developed within APPL-FM. Mounting and functional integration with TIAGo Pro are currently under way, initially on one side of the robot. In Robo4Care, SIRo investigates its potential for fine-motor tasks in ambulatory care. In SIRo-AEMP, the hand is being evaluated for handling surgical instruments.

At the Robotics and Biology Laboratory, TU Berlin, he previously developed the RBO Hand 3, an anthropomorphic soft hand with sixteen independent degrees of actuation. His research there also examined in-hand manipulation under variations in object shape, weight and movement speed, and human grasping behaviour.

His doctoral research is conducted in cooperation with the Technical University of Munich, with Hannes Höppner as his BHT supervisor.
