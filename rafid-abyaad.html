Researcher

Rafid Abyaad

How does a robot learn movements from a person, and build tasks from them?

Building a library of movement primitives for dynamic manipulation, and composing tasks from it.

Rafid.Abyaad@bht-berlin.de · ORCID 0000-0002-8478-592X

Research focus

Rafid works in the tele.interaction project, mainly with the TIAGo Pro humanoid. His subject is manipulation tasks whose outcome is decided in a single instant. In throwing, that instant is the release: the position and the velocity of the hand at the moment the object leaves it determine where it lands, while the path taken to get there matters far less.

To capture this, he extends via-point movement primitives so that a via-point carries a target velocity alongside a target position and enforces both jointly. Candidate release states are sampled from a distribution learned through kinesthetic demonstration, checked against a ballistic model, and then used to condition the arm trajectory. No manual tuning is needed for each new target.

The wider aim is a library. Each skill the robot learns from demonstration becomes an entry that can be parameterised and reused, rather than a one-off script. Work on composition has begun and has produced no results yet. The idea is that large language models assemble a task from the primitives in the library, so that a task given in words can be carried out without hand-coding the sequence.
