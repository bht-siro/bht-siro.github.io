TIAGo Pro

The robot that carries the rest: our hands on its arms, our input station at its controls, our movement primitives in its repertoire.

The platform at the centre

TIAGo Pro by PAL Robotics is a dual-arm mobile humanoid with two 7-DoF torque-controlled arms (built on series elastic actuators), an omnidirectional base, and a lifting torso. In our lab, TIAGo is meant to become something more specific: the last two wrist axes replaced by a SIRo Soft Hand, giving the robot compliant, variable-stiffness grasping where the mechanical design does much of the work that would otherwise fall to the controller.

This modified TIAGo is the system we teleoperate, the system we deploy into scenarios, and the system students work with from the second semester. It ties our research together (soft robotic hands, teleoperation, and the dynamic skills it learns from demonstration) into a single, tangible platform.

SIRo Soft Hand integration

planned Three SIRo Soft Hands exist, the right one nearly finished. Mounting them on TIAGo Pro is the next step and is planned for this year. Until then the robot works with its standard grippers, and everything below describes the design, not the current state of the machine.

Each of TIAGo Pro’s arms has seven degrees of freedom and normally ends in a standard gripper. Our hand does not need the last two of them, because it brings a wrist of its own. That wrist is not an addition but a necessity: the variable stiffness has to act there, and the motors driving it would not otherwise fit into the forearm.

The intended configuration therefore takes wrist roll and pitch off the robot and puts the hand, with its own compliant wrist, in their place. The arm then contributes five controlled degrees of freedom, and what comes back in exchange is variable-stiffness fingers, passive adaptation, and the ability to grasp without explicit force control.

The same compliant end effector is meant to serve very different tasks: picking tomatoes in a greenhouse, handing objects to a caregiver, or executing a throw learned from human demonstration. Of those, only the throw exists today, and it was performed with a 7-DoF arm and the standard gripper, not with the hand.

One platform, three application domains

We deploy TIAGo in three distinct contexts. Each one tests different aspects of compliant telemanipulation, but they all share the same hardware.

tele.interaction · DFG

Greenhouse

Teleoperating TIAGo to harvest tomatoes, handle plants, and navigate cluttered rows. The greenhouse is our primary research scenario, where spatial configuration, manipulability, and compliant grasping all come together.

Robo4Care · IFAF

Ambulatory Care

Together with care providers we are working out which everyday fine-motor tasks compliant teleoperation can support, and which it cannot. Handling small objects and preparing medication are among the candidates being assessed. The answer is what the project is meant to produce.

B.Sc. HROB

Teaching

TIAGo Pro takes an active role in courses rather than serving as a demonstration. In agile project exercises it acts as scrum master; in the ball pointing game it is a participant. Student challenges use the robot for the same tasks we address in our research, including the Catch-Up Challenge.

Teleoperation via VHIS

TIAGo is the robot side of our tele.interaction setup. On the operator side sits the Visuo-Haptic Input Station (VHIS), a dual-arm station with visual and haptic feedback that lets a human control TIAGo’s compliant hands with intuitive, embodied input.

The central research question is how compliant hardware on the robot side changes what a remote operator can do. When the hand adapts passively to the shape of an object, the operator does not have to plan individual finger motions and can concentrate on the task while the morphology handles the grip.
