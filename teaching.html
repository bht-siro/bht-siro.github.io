Teaching

We connect robotics research with teaching through design projects, experiments and work on research platforms. Students develop robotic hands and investigate manipulation tasks that also inform the lab’s research.

Learning with research hardware

The lab contributes to BHT’s B.Eng. Humanoid Robotics. Students work on mechanical design, actuation and control through their own prototypes and the lab’s research platforms, including TIAGo Pro.

Open research questions provide topics for projects and theses. Student experiments contribute observations and design experience that inform further development in the lab.

The Robotics Project

In the Robotics Project, teams design, build and test a soft robotic hand over one semester. A challenge evaluates their designs using tasks from an external benchmark or a research application.

The 2023 challenge used the task catalogue of the Cybathlon Arm Prosthesis Race unchanged. In 2024, teams built hands and one-handed controllers for tomato harvesting and plant maintenance in the BHT greenhouse. These tasks connect to the DFG tele.interaction project and introduce questions of hand design and operator control also addressed by our input station.

We are planning a separate teaching scenario with TIAGo Pro as Scrum Master or team member in the Ball Point Game. The research objective is to examine how robotic participation affects collaboration, learning motivation and understanding of agile methods. See the application overview.

From design decisions to experimental evidence

Students practise the full engineering process: translating task requirements into a design, building a prototype and evaluating its performance. Experiments connect decisions about geometry, materials and actuation to observed behaviour.

The greenhouse challenges provide one example. The tested hands could pick individual tomatoes but lacked the grip force to operate harvesting scissors. Such observations help students identify design limits and formulate the next iteration.
