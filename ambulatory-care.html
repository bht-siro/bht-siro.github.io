Care Robotics

We develop robotic manipulation and teleoperation systems for research in ambulatory care. Our focus is medication preparation and other fine-motor handling tasks in the context of care at home.

Teleoperated medication preparation

Within Robo4Care, we develop compliant manipulation and teleoperation for repetitive handling tasks in ambulatory care. Medication preparation provides a concrete setting for investigating precision, object variability and operator assistance.

1

Compliant manipulation

We are equipping TIAGo Pro with a SIRo Soft Hand, initially on one side, and developing its functional integration with a compact input station. Medication preparation is a target workflow, including removing tablets from blister packs and sorting them into organisers. Planned studies with our practice partners will investigate the system’s capabilities and limits.

Our research concerns hardware and teleoperation. The lab does not develop medical devices or diagnostic systems.

2

Operator interface

A compact input device is designed to let the operator guide the robot through corresponding arm movements. We are developing this interface for use with the manipulation system, so that care professionals can contribute task knowledge without programming individual robot motions.

Project partners

Robo4Care is funded by the State of Berlin through IFAF Berlin’s Verbund programme from January 2026 to December 2027. It brings together three universities of applied sciences, research organisations and practice partners. The wider project considers both residential and ambulatory care, with studies planned in laboratories and partner facilities.

State of Berlin · IFAF Verbund · 01/2026–12/2027 ASH Berlin HTW Berlin BHT Johannesstift Diakonie IFA / DGUV Fraunhofer IZM / IPK Deutsche Rentenversicherung Bund Berlin Partner

ASH Berlin contributes nursing science and study design; HTW Berlin develops measurement methods and equipment. At BHT, SIRo Lab develops teleoperated manipulation systems, while another research laboratory addresses exoskeleton comfort. Johannesstift Diakonie contributes care expertise and supports the selection and assessment of application scenarios.

Evaluating manipulation in care

Under what conditions can a teleoperated robot support medication preparation in ambulatory care? The planned evaluation considers task success, handling errors, time requirements and manual intervention across different tablets and packaging. Together with care professionals, we aim to assess technical feasibility, usability and compatibility with care workflows.
