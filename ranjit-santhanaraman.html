Researcher

Ranjit Santhanaraman

How does someone with no training in operating a humanoid control it with ease, and feel what the robot feels?

A haptic input station built from two cobot arms, for teleoperating humanoid robots.

Ranjit.Santhanaraman@bht-berlin.de · ORCID 0009-0008-0842-4541

Research focus

Ranjit develops a haptic input station for teleoperating humanoid robots. It is built from two Franka Research 3 cobot arms meant to serve two purposes at once: tracking the movement of the operator’s arms, and rendering the forces at the robot’s hand back, so that the operator feels what the remote robot touches. Published so far is the method for placing the arms around the operator. Commanding a robot arm from a cobot arm and returning haptic feedback have each been shown internally, on a single arm.

A station like this only works if the arms are placed well around the operator. His work models the reachable space of a human arm as a continuous mesh, built from forward kinematics with configuration-dependent joint limits rather than from inverse-kinematics solutions, intersects it with the reachable space of the cobot, and derives from that overlap where the arm should stand; the second arm is mirrored. Two measures he introduced, an arm closeness index and a joint closeness index, make competing configurations comparable rather than a matter of intuition.

The station is the operator end of the tele.interaction project and is used to teleoperate TIAGo Pro with the lab’s compliant hands.
