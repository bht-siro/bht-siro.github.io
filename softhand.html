SIRo Soft Hand

How much of the control can the hardware itself take over?

Mechanical intelligence through soft robotics

Soft robotic hands leverage passive deformation of their inherently compliant hardware during interaction with their environment and with humans to prevent damage and improve safety. Furthermore, deliberate exploitation of passive deformation has been shown to substantially reduce demands on accuracy in sensing, modelling and actuation. Based on these principles, compliant robots demonstrate complex yet reliable behaviours despite following simple strategies, for example when the morphology of a soft hand passively adapts to the object, resulting in robust grasping and manipulation.

Following this premise, soft robots effectively outsource relevant aspects of low-level control from software to hardware, as the soft body implicitly realises mechanical impedance control. In most soft hands, the level of compliance is directly coupled to posture, which can result in undesirable passive responses, for example when soft fingers bend away from the object during grasping.

Variable stiffness for dexterous manipulation

The ability to adjust compliance levels independently from posture during operation can profoundly enhance the capabilities and performance of soft systems. For example, a soft gripper in a low stiffness mode passively conforms to the contours of a delicate object, before stiffness increases to securely constrain and grasp the object without damaging it.

The SIRo Soft Hand serves as a reliable and versatile research platform for investigating the benefits of variable stiffness on contact-rich interaction with the environment, dexterous in-hand manipulation and safe human-robot interaction.

Building on it

The VSB mechanism is described in full in our ICRA 2025 paper: the geometry, the off-the-shelf components, the analytical model and the measurements it was validated against. Anyone who wants to reproduce it, adapt it to a different finger or argue with it has everything needed in that paper. The publisher holds the full text; we do not host a copy.

The hand as a whole is a research platform under active development, and the part of it that goes into SIRo-AEMP is on a path towards a possible spin-off from BHT.
