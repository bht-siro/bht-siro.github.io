SIRo Soft Hand

How much of the control can the hardware itself take over?

Fingertip deformation ↓ Variable-stiffness actuation ↓

Compliant mechanics for manipulation

We develop soft robotic hands whose mechanics contribute to grasping and manipulation. Compliant fingers deform on contact and adapt to an object’s shape, reducing the need to specify every contact through sensing and control. Our research examines how this passive response can be designed for a task, and where additional control is needed.

Morphological computation

Programming fingertip deformation

A soft fingertip can conform to an object or bend away from it. We shape this response by varying the thickness of its lattice structure, creating regions with different compliance. The deformation is programmed into the material geometry during fabrication and responds passively to contact.

Our study compared five compliance profiles in human-operated grasping and manipulation tasks. Selected combinations of softer and firmer regions improved performance compared with uniformly soft fingertips. This illustrates morphological computation: the physical structure itself contributes to the task.

VSB · Variable Stiffness based on Bowden Cables

Adjusting how a joint yields to contact

The VSB mechanism combines opposing tendon drives, Bowden cables and compression springs to adjust a joint’s mechanical stiffness. Increasing preload compresses the spring series until individual springs reach their limits, producing distinct stiffness modes. The cable transmission allows the motors and elastic elements to be placed away from the joint, supporting compact hand designs.

The published experiments evaluate a single-joint mechanism with a set of stiffness modes. We characterised its passive deflection and contact torques, and compared analytical and data-driven models. This work provides a basis for investigating adjustable compliance in the SIRo Soft Hand.

From mechanisms to manipulation

Fingertip geometry shapes the local response at the object, while variable-stiffness actuation adjusts compliance at the joint. The SIRo Soft Hand brings these research directions together as a platform for contact-rich manipulation.

The hand remains under active development. SIRo-AEMP investigates its application to surgical instrument handling and contributes to a possible spin-off from BHT.
