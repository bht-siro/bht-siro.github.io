Surgical Instrument Reprocessing

After every operation, the instruments used have to be reprocessed. The work is monotonous and carries a real risk of injury and infection. We are testing whether a compliant robotic hand can take it over.

The task

In a sterile processing department (in German: Aufbereitungseinheit für Medizinprodukte, AEMP), contaminated instruments arrive in sieve trays, often tangled and in no particular order. Before machine cleaning they have to be identified, checked, opened where they have a joint, and racked so that every surface is reachable by the washer.

In SIRo-AEMP we equip a robot arm with a SIRo Soft Hand and a camera and give it exactly this job: pick individual instruments out of the tray, open scissors and retractors, and place them in a controlled and secure way into the containers in which they are sterilised.

1

Grasping what is tangled

Instruments arrive interlocked and unsorted. Separating them requires a hand that tolerates contact and adapts to unplanned geometry rather than one that needs a known grasp pose.

2

Seeing what reflects

Polished steel is a hard case for camera-based perception. Recognising and localising highly reflective instruments reliably is one of the central technical questions of the project.

3

Handling what is sharp

Scalpels, sharp tips and cutting edges have to be handled without damaging the instrument, the gripper or anything else. Compliance helps here, but it has to be paired with deliberate motion planning.

4

Opening what is hinged

Hinged instruments only get clean if they are opened first. Doing that reliably needs the fine-motor capability the SIRo Soft Hand was built for: fingers, a thumb and a movable wrist.

Why this project matters to the lab

SIRo-AEMP is a validation project. Years of work on variable stiffness, Bowden-cable actuation and programmable fingertip compliance have produced a hand that performs well in the lab. Here it has to hold up under realistic conditions, against a task defined by a clinical process rather than by us.

That makes it a useful stress test in both directions. It shows which of our design decisions survive contact with an industrial workflow, and it feeds concrete requirements back into the next generation of the SIRo Soft Hand. The project also prepares the ground for a possible spin-off from BHT.

Funding and partners

SIRo-AEMP is funded by the State of Berlin through Investitionsbank Berlin (IBB) under ProValid, the programme for validating research results. The project started in April 2026. Industry partner is enamentis GmbH, which specialises in the automation of sterile goods logistics.
