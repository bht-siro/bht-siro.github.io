Research in four application areas

We investigate how robotic manipulation and human-robot interaction can support plant handling, care, surgical instrument reprocessing and university teaching.

Each application introduces different requirements for the robot, the task and its users. Our work ranges from student experiments and early grasping trials to system development; the status labels distinguish these stages.

Plant handling and instrument reprocessing emphasise manipulation. The classroom provides a setting for studying human-robot interaction, while care combines manipulation with teleoperation and workflow requirements.

Teaching applications

planned scenario · B.Eng. Humanoide Robotik

We plan to investigate TIAGo Pro as a Scrum Master or team member in the Ball Point Game. The scenario will examine how robot participation influences collaboration and students’ understanding of agile project management. It has not yet been used in teaching.

Our teaching also connects student engineering projects with the lab’s research.

Surgical instrument reprocessing

first trials · SIRo-AEMP, with enamentis GmbH

We investigate grasping, separating and opening surgical instruments in preparation for machine cleaning. Reflective surfaces, interlocked objects and sharp edges create challenges for perception and manipulation. First grasps have been demonstrated; a complete instrument-handling sequence remains a development goal.

The project in detail →

The greenhouse

first student experiments · DFG tele.interaction

Tomato cultivation requires delicate fruit and plant handling. Student-built hands successfully picked individual fruits, while de-suckering and defoliation caused plant damage. In two challenge rounds, the tested hands lacked sufficient grip force to close harvesting scissors.

Experiments with TIAGo Pro in the greenhouse are planned. The scenario in detail →

Care Robotics

in development · Robo4Care, IFAF Berlin

Within Robo4Care, we develop teleoperation and grasping systems to investigate medication preparation in ambulatory care. A partner laboratory studies wearable exoskeletons for physically demanding care work.

Our research focuses on technical feasibility and the requirements of care at home. The project in detail →

From application requirements to research

These settings connect technical development with task requirements and user needs. Observations from experiments inform the next design steps: the greenhouse challenges, for example, identified insufficient grip force for operating the tested scissors.
