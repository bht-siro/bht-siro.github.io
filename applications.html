Where compliant manipulation has to hold up

Four settings, four different states of progress. A hand that works on the bench is not yet a hand that works among tomato plants or surgical instruments.

In the laboratory we choose the objects, the tolerances and the people, so almost anything can be made to work. In the four settings below we choose none of them, and that is why they are here. They differ in how far the work has come, and the tag on each one says where it stands.

Three of them are research domains in the usual sense. The fourth, the classroom, is one we are in every week anyway, which makes it the setting where a machine gets used most and excused least.

The classroom

running · B.Sc. Humanoide Robotik

TIAGo Pro appears as a scrum master in agile project exercises and as a participant in the ball pointing game. Students work alongside a real humanoid rather than reading about one, and a robot handled by a whole cohort each week runs into failures that a planned laboratory session rarely produces.

This is the robot used as a tool in a setting. How the lab teaches, and how student work feeds back into research, is a different matter and is described under teaching.

Surgical instrument reprocessing

first trials · SIRo-AEMP, with enamentis GmbH

Instruments arrive tangled, they reflect, they are sharp, and the hinged ones only get clean if they are opened first. First grasps have been demonstrated, and that is a long way from a workflow that a sterile processing department could rely on.

The project in detail →

The greenhouse

first student experiments · DFG tele.interaction

Ripe tomatoes, leaves and stems are soft, irregular and easily bruised, which is what compliant hands are for. Student teams have already worked in the BHT greenhouse and found the limit: picking single fruits works, de-suckering and defoliating damaged the plants, and the grip force was not enough to close a pair of scissors, twice.

The lab robot itself has not been in the greenhouse yet. The scenario in detail →

Ambulatory care

running · Robo4Care, IFAF Berlin

Care work is strenuous in two different ways, and a single technology rarely helps with both. We pair compliant manipulation for fine-motor tasks with wearable exoskeletons for the physically demanding ones, and the question we are actually working on is which tasks are suitable at all.

The lab researches hardware and teleoperation. It does not develop medical devices. The project in detail →

What these four have in common

None of them is a benchmark we wrote. Each brings a partner who knows the setting better than we do, and each has produced at least one result we did not expect. The greenhouse told us that grip force, not compliance, is the binding constraint. That kind of answer is the reason for going out of the building at all.
