Theses and internships

Contribute to research on robotic mechanisms, movement learning or teleoperation through a thesis, internship or student project.

Student research at SIRo Lab

We develop student projects around the lab’s four research areas. Work can involve mechanical design, modelling, software development or experiments. Each project addresses a defined question and documents the methods, findings and limitations.

The Robotics Project provides an introduction to this approach. In its greenhouse challenges, student-built hands successfully picked individual tomatoes but lacked sufficient force to operate harvesting scissors. These observations help identify requirements for further hand development.

Theses and internships contribute to the development of research prototypes and methods. Results can provide a basis for subsequent projects and scientific publications.

Research areas for student projects

Let us know which area interests you. We can then discuss a suitable question and scope in relation to your background, available time and current research needs.

How much of the control can the hardware itself take over?

Mechanical Intelligence

Variable stiffness, programmable fingertip compliance and the role of mechanics in grasping. Methods include mechanical design, additive manufacturing and experimental measurement.

What becomes possible when elasticity spans more than one joint?

Stiffness Shaping

Multiarticular couplings, Cartesian stiffness and energy transfer between joints. Methods include mechanical design, simulation and control.

How does a robot learn movements from a person, and build tasks from them?

Movement Primitives

Learning from demonstration, via-point movement primitives and research on task composition through language models. Methods include programming, machine learning and robot experiments.

How does someone with no training in operating a humanoid control it with ease, and feel what the robot feels?

Immersive Teleoperation

Haptic input stations, workspace configuration and feedback for remote manipulation. Methods include control, human factors and user studies.

Enquire about available topics

Contact us to discuss current opportunities in these research areas. Topics are defined individually, taking account of research priorities, your interests and supervision capacity.

How to enquire

Send a short email to hannes.hoeppner@bht-berlin.de, including:

  • The research area or question that interests you.
  • The type of opportunity: bachelor’s thesis, master’s thesis, internship or student assistant position.
  • Your intended start date and available time.
  • Your relevant experience, for example in hardware, CAD, programming, control, machine learning or user studies.

An initial area of interest is sufficient; a complete project proposal and experience in every field are not required.

Enquiries from students at other universities and in other programmes are welcome. Opportunities depend on the topic and available supervision capacity.

What you can expect

  • Work with the lab’s research platforms.
  • Supervision by the person working on that question, and by the lab director.
  • Workshop access in the Haus der Robotik, including laser cutting, milling and additive manufacturing.
  • Opportunities to contribute to scientific publications, depending on the work and the individual contribution.

Research outcomes are open. A carefully documented limitation or unsuccessful approach can be a useful contribution when it answers the project’s question.
