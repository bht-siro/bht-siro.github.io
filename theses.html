Theses and internships

Where topics come from, what an enquiry should contain, and who answers.

What working here looks like

Student work in this lab is not a separate track running alongside the research. Topics are cut out of the four questions the lab is working on, which means the answer is not known in advance, by anyone. That is the appeal and the risk at the same time: a result may be that something does not work, and that result is worth just as much.

The Robotics Project in the bachelor programme is where most people meet the lab first. Teams build a soft robotic hand and put it to a real test, and what comes out of that has repeatedly changed what the lab does next. The greenhouse challenges showed that picking fruit works and that the grip force is not sufficient to close a pair of scissors. Nobody had planned that finding, and it is now part of how we think about hand design.

Prototypes that hold up become lab platforms. Results from theses turn into the groundwork of the next paper. A lab of this size can only carry four research questions at once because students carry part of them.

The four fields topics come from

Every topic sits under one of these. If one of them is what you want to spend six months on, that is the right thing to say in your first email.

How much of the control can the hardware itself take over?

Mechanical Intelligence

Variable stiffness, programmable fingertip compliance, grasping without explicit force control. Mechanical design, additive manufacturing, measurement.

What becomes possible when elasticity spans more than one joint?

Stiffness Shaping

Multiarticular couplings, Cartesian stiffness ellipses, energy transfer between joints. Mechanics, simulation, control.

How does a robot learn movements from a person, and build tasks from them?

Movement Primitives

Learning from demonstration, via-point primitives, a library of movements, composition through language models. Programming, machine learning, experiments on the robot.

How does someone with no training in operating a humanoid control it with ease, and feel what the robot feels?

Immersive Teleoperation

Haptic input stations, workspace configuration, what has to be fed back for contact to arrive as contact. Control, human factors, user studies.

Open topics

Currently advertised topics will be listed here. Until they are, an email describing which of the four fields interests you is the better route, because most topics are cut to fit the person rather than the other way round.

How to ask

Write to hannes.hoeppner@bht-berlin.de. A short email is enough, and these four things make it much easier to answer:

  • Which of the four fields above interests you, and why that one.
  • What kind of work you are looking for: bachelor thesis, master thesis, internship, or a student assistant position.
  • When you would like to start, and how much time you have.
  • What you can already do. Hardware, CAD, Python, C++, control, machine learning, experiments with people. Nobody is expected to bring all of it.

You do not need a finished topic proposal. You do need a rough idea of which question you want to sit with.

Students from outside BHT and from other programmes are welcome to ask. Whether it works depends on supervision capacity at the time, so the honest answer may be no.

What you can expect

  • Access to the platforms the lab actually researches with, not a teaching replica.
  • Supervision by the person working on that question, and by the lab director.
  • Workshop access in the Haus der Robotik: laser cutting, milling, and several printing processes on the ground floor.
  • If the result carries, co-authorship on a publication. That is not a promise, it is what has happened.

What the lab cannot offer is a topic with a guaranteed outcome. See the first paragraph.
